## Executive summary

* Treat “deduction style” as a **calculus profile** that sits alongside (not inside) your existing notion of a **logic** (CPL/IPC/LL/STLC…) and a **rule set**. A theorem selects a *profile*; a profile selects a *judgment form* + allowed rules + UI affordances.
* Implement three judgment forms in the kernel:

  1. **Natural Deduction (ND)**: derivations with **assumption discharge** (scoped hypotheses). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])
  2. **Hilbert/Frege**: **linear proofs** (sequence of formulas) using **axiom schemata + Modus Ponens** (+ quantifier mechanisms for FO). ([us.metamath.org][2])
  3. **Gentzen sequent calculus LK**: derivations of **sequents** Γ ⇒ Δ with explicit **structural + logical rules + Cut** (Cut-elimination is a meta-theorem; do not build it into the kernel). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* Represent rules uniformly as **schematic inferences** (pattern + premises + substitution + side-conditions). Side-conditions (fresh/eigenvariable, “free for” substitution) must be first-class and produce explainable diagnostics. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* Add translations as **proof transformers** between profiles (ND↔LK, Hilbert→ND/LK, plus your existing logic-to-logic translations). Expect proof-size blowups when eliminating Cut or simulating discharge in Hilbert; cut-elimination can be exponential (propositional) and non-elementary (first-order). ([web2.qatar.cmu.edu][4])
* UX: expose the calculus profile at **workspace/file/theorem** granularity; render proof state differently per profile (ND: assumptions/subproofs; Hilbert: line references; LK: sequent with left/right contexts). Provide “import/translate proof” with preview + warnings.

## Rule tables

### Notation used

* Formulas: A,B,C; contexts: Γ,Δ,Π,Λ (lists/multisets as chosen by the profile).
* ND judgment: derivation of A from undischarged assumptions Γ (often written Γ ⊢ A).
* LK judgment: sequent Γ ⇒ Δ (read: from all Γ, at least one Δ). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* “Eigenvariable/eigenconstant” conditions are required for sound quantifier rules. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

---

### 1) Natural Deduction (ND)

#### Propositional ND (minimal complete core)

The following Gentzen/Prawitz-style ND rules are a standard core for propositional logic. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])

| Connective | Introduction                                                        | Elimination                                                    | Context / discharge notes                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ∧          | from A and B infer A ∧ B (∧Intro)                                   | from A ∧ B infer A (∧Elim₁) and infer B (∧Elim₂)               | No discharge. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])                                                              |
| ∨          | from A infer A ∨ B; from B infer A ∨ B (∨Intro)                     | from A ∨ B plus subderivations A ⟹ C and B ⟹ C infer C (∨Elim) | **Discharges** the temporary assumptions A and B used in the two branches. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1]) |
| →          | from a subderivation assuming A and deriving B infer A → B (→Intro) | from A → B and A infer B (→Elim / MP)                          | →Intro **discharges** the assumed A. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])                                       |
| ¬          | from a subderivation assuming A and deriving ⊥ infer ¬A (¬Intro)    | from ¬A and A infer ⊥ (¬Elim)                                  | ¬Intro **discharges** A. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])                                                   |
| ⊥          | (often) from ⊥ infer any A (⊥C / explosion)                         | (optional) define ⊥I via ¬A + A                                | Explosion is explicit in this presentation. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])                                |

**Classical vs intuitionistic ND**

* The above is typically **intuitionistic/minimal** unless you add a classical principle.
* Common minimal additions to obtain **classical** propositional logic:

  * **Double-negation elimination**: ¬¬A ⟹ A, or
  * **Law of excluded middle**: A ∨ ¬A, or
  * **RAA (classical reductio)**: from (¬A ⟹ ⊥) infer A.
    Gentzen’s original discussion distinguishes classical and intuitionistic calculi and motivates sequent calculi partly to study cut-elimination uniformly. ([logic-teaching.github.io][5])

#### What changes for first-order ND

Add quantifier rules (plus side-conditions). A standard Gentzen/Prawitz-style set is: ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])

| Quantifier | Introduction                     | Elimination                                                               | Side conditions (kernel must check)                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ∀          | from φ(a) infer ∀x φ(x) (∀Intro) | from ∀x φ(x) infer φ(t) (∀Elim)                                           | ∀Intro requires **fresh** eigenconstant a not occurring in the conclusion or any **undischarged** assumption used to derive φ(a). ∀Elim typically requires **closed** term t in this presentation. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1]) |
| ∃          | from φ(t) infer ∃x φ(x) (∃Intro) | from ∃x φ(x) and a subderivation assuming φ(a) deriving C infer C (∃Elim) | ∃Elim requires fresh eigenconstant a not occurring in the premise ∃xφ(x), the conclusion C, or any undischarged assumptions used in the other premise derivation; also discharge φ(a). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])             |

**Proof object shape (ND):** a tree with explicit **subproof blocks** (scoped assumptions) and explicit discharge sites (→Intro, ¬Intro, ∨Elim, ∃Elim). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])

### 2) Frege/Hilbert-style systems

Hilbert systems trade many introduction/elimination rules for **axiom schemata** and very few rules (often only MP; for FO add generalization or quantifier schemata).

#### Minimal complete Hilbert system for classical propositional logic (CPL)

A widely used 3-axiom-schema basis with MP is: ([us.metamath.org][2])

* **Axiom schema 1 (Simp):**  φ → (ψ → φ) ([us.metamath.org][2])
* **Axiom schema 2 (Frege / distribution):** (φ → (ψ → χ)) → ((φ → ψ) → (φ → χ)) ([us.metamath.org][6])
* **Axiom schema 3 (Classical via contraposition form):** (¬φ → ¬ψ) → (ψ → φ) ([us.metamath.org][7])
* **Rule (MP):** from φ and (φ → ψ) infer ψ ([us.metamath.org][8])

**Common variants**

* Replace axiom 3 with **Peirce’s law** `((φ→ψ)→φ)→φ` (still classical), or add **double-negation elimination** as the classical axiom.
* Use implication-only fragments (complete only for the implicational fragment): e.g., a 2-axiom basis for → plus MP appears as a basic Hilbert system H₁. ([www3.cs.stonybrook.edu][9])

#### Intuitionistic propositional logic (IPL/IPC) in Hilbert style

One practical Hilbert-style axiomatization for intuitionistic propositional logic (with ∪, ∩, ⇒, ¬) uses axioms A1–A11 plus MP. ([www3.cs.stonybrook.edu][10])
This is larger than “minimal” axiom sets, but it is implementable and common in course material; it is explicitly presented as a Hilbert formalization for intuitionistic logic and notes equivalence to Heyting-style systems. ([www3.cs.stonybrook.edu][10])

#### What changes for first-order Hilbert systems

You need a mechanism for quantifiers. Two standard patterns:

1. **Axiom schemata for ∀ plus MP**, with side-conditions about substitution (“free for”) and non-freeness of variables, plus a **generalization rule**. Metamath lists traditional predicate calculus schemes and conditions, including:

* ∀x φ(x) → φ(y) provided y is free for x in φ
* ∀x(φ → ψ) → (φ → ∀x ψ) provided x is not free in φ
* Generalization: from ⊢ φ infer ⊢ ∀x φ ([us.metamath.org][11])

2. Alternatively: keep fewer quantifier axioms but add an explicit **Gen** rule with the usual restriction (“x not free in any undischarged assumption / hypothesis set”).

**Proof object shape (Hilbert):** a *linear sequence* of formulas, each justified as:

* an instance of an axiom schema, or
* a hypothesis (if you allow Γ ⊢ φ style derivations), or
* derived by MP from two earlier lines.

### 3) Gentzen sequent calculus LK (classical), and LJ (intuitionistic)

#### LK: minimal inventory (classical first-order sequent calculus)

Open Logic’s LK presentation provides the standard inventory. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Initial sequents**

* φ ⇒ φ
* ⇒ ⊤
* ⊥ ⇒ ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Logical rules (propositional)**

* ¬L / ¬R ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* ∧L (two projections) / ∧R ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* ∨L / ∨R (two injections) ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* →L / →R ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Quantifier rules**

* ∀L, ∀R with eigenvariable condition; ∃L, ∃R with eigenvariable condition and closed-term restrictions in some directions. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Structural rules**

* Weakening left/right (WL/WR)
* Contraction left/right (CL/CR)
* Exchange left/right (XL/XR) ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Cut**

* From Γ ⇒ Δ, φ and φ, Π ⇒ Λ infer Γ, Π ⇒ Δ, Λ. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Cut-elimination context**
Gentzen introduced LK/LJ to state and prove the Hauptsatz (cut-elimination) uniformly. ([logic-teaching.github.io][5])
Do not rely on cut-elimination operationally in your kernel; treat it as a meta-property used for automation/normalization strategies. Eliminating cut can cause exponential blowup in propositional logic and non-elementary blowup in first-order logic. ([web2.qatar.cmu.edu][4])

#### LJ (optional)

LJ is the intuitionistic counterpart: restrict the succedent to **at most one formula** and adjust rules accordingly. Gentzen emphasizes that classical/intuitionistic distinction in sequent calculus is expressed via succedent conditions. ([Wikipedia][12])

**Proof object shape (LK/LJ):** a tree of sequents; contexts are explicit on left/right; structural rules are explicit (unless you move to a structural-rule-free variant such as G3-style systems, which is a later design choice). ([CMU School of Computer Science][13])

### Differences in assumptions/context handling and proof shape (summary)

| Style   | Judgment                                      | Assumption handling                                                                                  | Typical proof shape                                                           |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ND      | Γ ⊢ A (often implicit Γ via open assumptions) | **Scoped assumptions** + **discharge**                                                               | Tree with nested subproof blocks ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])           |
| Hilbert | ⊢ A (or Γ ⊢ A)                                | No local discharge; any Γ is “global hypotheses” and is handled via Deduction Theorem meta-structure | Linear list; MP links lines ([us.metamath.org][8])                            |
| LK      | Γ ⇒ Δ                                         | Context is first-class on both sides; structural rules manage weakening/duplication/permutation      | Tree of sequents; Cut composes derivations ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3]) |

## Architecture proposal

### A) Uniform rule representation (“modular engine”)

#### 1) Core data model (kernel-facing)

**Formula/term layer**

* `Term`: variables, constants, function application.
* `Formula`: predicate atoms, connectives, quantifiers.
* Use either:

  * **locally nameless / de Bruijn** for bound vars (best for hygiene), or
  * named binders + alpha-equivalence normalization (harder but doable).
    Given eigenvariable and “free for substitution” constraints, strong hygiene is worth it.

**Judgments**
Model judgments as a tagged union with a shared interface:

```text
Judgment =
  | ND { context: CtxND, goal: Formula, scope: ScopeInfo }
  | Hilbert { hypotheses: CtxH, goal: Formula }
  | Sequent { left: CtxL, right: CtxR }    // LK/LJ
```

Key point: **Ctx must be parameterized** by structural discipline (you already have LL, so you cannot globally treat contexts as sets). For LK you want weakening+contraction+exchange available; for LL you may restrict them.

Practical approach:

* Define `ContextKind` describing admissible structural operations:

  * `Affine` (weakening), `Relevant` (contraction), `Linear` (neither), `Classical` (both).
* Each profile picks context discipline; rules declare which discipline they require.

#### 2) Rule objects: schematic inference + side conditions

Represent every rule as:

```text
Rule {
  id, family, display,
  conclusion: JudgmentPattern,
  premises: [JudgmentPattern],
  instantiate: (Subst) -> ProofNode,
  side_conditions: [SideCond],
  properties: { invertible?, focusingPhase?, structural?, classical_only?, ... }
}
```

* `JudgmentPattern` is a pattern with metavariables for formulas/terms/contexts.
* `Subst` is the unifier result (metas → AST fragments).
* `SideCond` is a small DSL evaluated after unification:

  * `FreshConst(c, avoid=...)` (eigenvariable)
  * `NotFreeIn(x, φ)` / `NotFreeIn(x, Γ)`
  * `FreeForSubst(t, x, φ)` (“t free for x in φ”) ([us.metamath.org][11])
  * “closed term” constraints (if using the OLP presentation). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

This gives you:

* modular rules,
* profile-defined rule sets,
* and diagnostics for side-condition failures.

#### 3) Proof objects

Keep proof objects uniform:

```text
Proof =
  Node { rule_id, instantiation: Subst, children: [Proof] , spans: [SourceSpan] }
```

Attach style-specific derived views:

* ND: pretty-print as Fitch / tree with discharged assumption labels.
* Hilbert: pretty-print as numbered list with “axiom instance / MP(i,j)”.
* LK: pretty-print as sequent tree with left/right contexts.

This also matches “small trusted kernel checks primitive rule applications” (common in practical assistants). ([opencourse.inf.ed.ac.uk][14])

---

### B) Layer separation

#### 1) Syntax / parsing layer

* Parse:

  * formulas/terms,
  * theorem headers (`theorem <name> using <Profile>`),
  * proof scripts with tactic invocations and explicit rule steps.
* Do not embed rule semantics here; only AST + spans.

#### 2) Rule semantics (kernel)

* Kernel checks a step by:

  1. loading the theorem’s selected profile,
  2. ensuring `rule_id ∈ profile.allowed_rules`,
  3. unifying goal with rule conclusion,
  4. checking side conditions,
  5. emitting subgoals (premises) and building a proof node.

#### 3) Proof search / tactic layer

* Tactics operate on the *profile interface*:

  * `list_applicable_rules(goal)`
  * `apply_rule(rule_id, args?)`
  * `refine(subgoal_idx, tactic)`
* Keep tactics profile-aware but not profile-hardcoded: tactics query rule metadata (e.g., “invertible” sequent rules) instead of checking `if profile == LK`.

#### 4) System profile declarations

Define profiles as data, not code:

```text
profile LK_CPL {
  logic: CPL,
  calculus: LK,
  context_discipline: Classical,
  rules: [init, ¬L, ¬R, ∧L1, ∧L2, …, Cut, WL, WR, CL, CR, XL, XR],
  ui: { renderer: "sequent", hide_structural_by_default: true }
}
```

This prevents “hardcoding system names in rule implementations.”

## Translation/interoperability design

### 1) ND ↔ Sequent calculus (LK/LJ)

#### ND → (LJ/LK) (structure-preserving embedding)

**Intuition:** ND’s intro/elim correspond to right/left rules; discharge corresponds to →R (or related right rules) and to the way assumptions are managed in the sequent. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])

**Algorithm sketch (constructive)**

* Translate a ND derivation of Γ ⊢ A into a sequent derivation of Γ ⇒ A (single succedent).
* Each ND rule maps to a small sequent derivation:

  * ∧Intro ↦ ∧R; ∧Elim ↦ ∧L + weakening/exchange as needed.
  * →Intro ↦ →R.
  * →Elim (MP) ↦ →L + (often) Cut if using a direct mapping; if you target cut-free sequent systems, use a structured translation (G3-style is often used for proof search). ([CMU School of Computer Science][13])

**Size expectations**

* Typically linear/polynomial for direct embeddings if you allow Cut where needed; targeting cut-free LK/LJ may increase size (sometimes substantially).

**Metadata to preserve**

* Assumption scopes/discharge sites (to reconstruct ND subproofs later),
* term instantiations for quantifiers,
* source spans for step mapping.

#### (LJ/LK) → ND

**Intuition:** A sequent derivation is already “goal-directed,” so it can often be re-expressed as ND. For LK (multi-succedent classical), translations may introduce classical steps (e.g., excluded middle) or use double-negation style embeddings. ([logic-teaching.github.io][5])

**Algorithm sketch**

* For LJ: translate Γ ⇒ A to ND Γ ⊢ A by structural recursion on the sequent proof (standard).
* For LK: either

  * translate to ND with explicit classical principles, or
  * translate LK to LJ via a classical-to-intuitionistic embedding and then to ND (but then you’re mixing with your existing logic translations).

A practical modern reference is an “efficient translation of LK proofs into ND” aimed at keeping excluded-middle instances low. ([Repositum][15])

**Size expectations**

* Often polynomial for LJ; LK→ND can be larger, especially if you first eliminate cuts or force single-succedent structure.

---

### 2) Hilbert/Frege → ND or LK

#### Hilbert → ND (recommended first)

**Intuition:** Every axiom instance becomes a tiny ND derivation; each MP step becomes →Elim in ND. ([us.metamath.org][8])

**Algorithm sketch**

* Input: Hilbert proof lines `L1..Ln`.
* Maintain a map `line i ↦ ND derivation Di` (possibly under hypotheses Γ if you support Γ ⊢).
* For each line:

  * If axiom instance: create fixed ND proof of that schema instance.
  * If hypothesis: use assumption rule (or a leaf).
  * If MP from j,k: build `Di := →Elim(Dj, Dk)` with correct ordering.
* Output: ND derivation for Ln.

This equivalence-by-induction is standard lecture-note level. ([Fnwi Staff][16])

**Size expectations**

* Typically linear overhead (each line yields a small ND fragment).

**Metadata to preserve**

* Which axiom schema was instantiated (for explanation),
* which line references justified MP (for reconstruction),
* substitution instances.

#### Hilbert → LK

**Intuition:** MP is a special case of Cut; axioms correspond to initial sequents + short derivations. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Algorithm sketch**

* Translate each Hilbert line to a sequent derivation of ⇒ φ (or Γ ⇒ φ).
* MP becomes Cut on φ. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* If you later want a cut-free LK proof, apply cut-elimination (separately).

**Size expectations**

* With Cut allowed: modest overhead.
* If you force cut-free: can explode (propositional exponential; first-order non-elementary). ([web2.qatar.cmu.edu][4])

---

### 3) ND → Hilbert (do later; expect blow-up)

**Intuition:** Use the Deduction Theorem to discharge assumptions in Hilbert style, reconstructing implication chains.

**Algorithm sketch**

* Induct on ND derivation:

  * For →Intro/¬Intro discharge, use the Deduction Theorem to move assumptions into implications.
  * For ∨Elim and ∃Elim, you typically need derived Hilbert lemmas that simulate case analysis / instantiation.
* This is implementable, but the library of derived lemmas matters.

**Size expectations**

* Often significantly larger than Hilbert→ND; case-analysis rules are expensive in Hilbert style.

## UX proposal

### A) How users choose/change proof style

**Configuration hierarchy**

1. Workspace default: `defaultProfile = ND_IPC` (or similar)
2. File directive: `#profile LK_CPL` at top (optional)
3. Theorem-level override (already supported):
   `theorem Foo using LK_CPL`

**Changing style**

* Provide a command: “Change theorem profile…”

  * If translation exists: offer **Translate proof** (with preview).
  * Otherwise: change header only and mark existing proof as incompatible.

### B) Mixed projects (different theorems, different systems)

* Show a small badge near each theorem: `ND/IPC`, `LK/CPL`, `Hilbert/IPC`.
* Project tree filter: by logic (IPC/CPL/…) and by calculus (ND/Hilbert/LK).
* Cross-theorem reuse:

  * When applying lemma L in a theorem T of different profile, offer:

    * “Use via translation” (if available), or
    * “Import statement only” (no proof).

### C) Proof UI per style

#### 1) ND UI

* **Context pane:** tree/stack of open assumptions; highlight discharged assumptions at the rule site. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])
* **Step editor:** subproof blocks with indentation; discharge markers on →Intro/¬Intro/∨Elim/∃Elim.
* **Rule palette:** grouped by connective; show Intro/Elim pairs.

#### 2) Hilbert UI

* **Main view:** numbered line list; each line has:

  * formula,
  * justification: `Axiom ax-2 (instantiation …)` or `MP i j`.
* **Palette:** mostly “Add axiom instance” + “Apply MP (select two lines)”.
* **Ergonomics:** add “macro rules” (derived rules) as *tactics* (not kernel rules) to avoid extremely low-level proofs.

#### 3) LK UI

* **Goal view:** render sequent as two panes:

  * left Γ (assumptions/resources),
  * right Δ (goals/disjunction).
* **Rule palette:** show *left* and *right* versions of each connective; optionally hide structural rules behind an “Advanced” accordion (but keep them available).
* **Interaction model:** user selects a **focus formula** in Γ or Δ; palette filters to rules that can act on it.
* **Structural rules UX:** provide quick actions:

  * “Reorder” (exchange),
  * “Duplicate” (contraction),
  * “Add assumption/goal” (weakening),
    but keep them as explicit steps if your kernel requires explicit structural steps (important because you already support LL where these are not admissible).

### D) “Import proof from A to B”

* Wizard:

  1. Source theorem/profile
  2. Target profile
  3. Compatibility checks (logic translation vs calculus translation)
  4. Preview:

     * estimated step count,
     * warnings: “may introduce classical axiom”, “cut-elimination may blow up” ([web2.qatar.cmu.edu][4])
  5. Produce transformed proof with an explanation trace (rule-by-rule mapping).

### E) Error/diagnostic UX for side conditions

Side conditions should report:

* **What failed:** e.g., “eigenconstant `a` occurs in undischarged assumption Γ” (∀Intro/∃Elim) ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])
* **Where:** highlight occurrences of `a` in the proof state.
* **Fix actions:**

  * “Rename eigenconstant” (auto-suggest fresh),
  * “Close subproof / discharge assumption” (if applicable),
  * “Use different term (closed term required)” where relevant. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

## Migration plan (incremental roadmap)

### M1: Introduce LK (propositional fragment)

**Kernel/data model**

* Add `Judgment.Sequent {left,right}` + context discipline (classical structural rules enabled).
* Implement LK propositional rules: ¬,∧,∨,→ plus initial sequents, structural rules, and Cut. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Parser/UI**

* Add sequent goal rendering and focus-selection.
* Add “apply sequent rule” step form (pick rule + focus formula).

**Testing**

* Golden proofs for small tautologies (excluded middle, modus ponens as sequent, distributivity).
* Rule law tests:

  * local soundness sanity checks by random small valuations for propositional sequents (cheap),
  * regression tests for exchange/weakening/contraction behavior.

**Pitfalls**

* Do not represent LK contexts as sets globally (breaks LL extensibility).
* Make structural steps explicit in proof objects even if UI hides them.

---

### M2: Add Hilbert profile (propositional)

**Kernel/data model**

* Add `Judgment.Hilbert {hypotheses, goal}` or model as `⊢ goal` with a proof script that is a line list.
* Implement classical 3-axiom basis (ax-1..ax-3) + MP as a profile. ([us.metamath.org][2])
* Add an intuitionistic Hilbert profile (larger axiom set is acceptable initially for implementability). ([www3.cs.stonybrook.edu][10])

**Parser/UI**

* New proof view: numbered lines.
* Justification editor: choose “axiom instance” or “MP from i,j”.

**Testing**

* Golden line-proofs for simple theorems (A→A, etc.) referencing known derivations.
* Schema-instantiation tests: unification/substitution must be capture-avoiding at the formula-variable level.

**Pitfalls**

* Ambiguity between object-level variables vs meta-variables in schemata: keep them separate (schema vars are not term vars).

---

### M3: Add first-order quantifiers + side conditions (ND + LK + Hilbert)

**Kernel/data model**

* Introduce `Term` language, binding representation, capture-avoiding substitution.
* Implement ND quantifier rules with eigenvariable conditions. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][1])
* Implement LK quantifier rules with eigenvariable conditions and term restrictions (per your chosen presentation). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
* For Hilbert FO, add generalization + ∀-schemata (or equivalent), with “free for” constraints. ([us.metamath.org][11])

**Parser/UI**

* Term syntax, binder pretty-printing, alpha-equivalence aware error messages.
* Diagnostics UI for eigenvariable/free-for errors.

**Testing**

* Unit tests for substitution hygiene (no capture), alpha-equivalence, freshness generation.
* Golden proofs that specifically stress eigenvariable conditions (both passing and failing cases shown in references). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

**Pitfalls**

* If you allow “closed terms only” in eliminations (as in the cited presentation), document it; it affects ergonomics and translation.

---

### M4: Add proof translation commands

**Kernel/data model**

* Add `Translator { srcProfile, dstProfile, transform(proof) }` registry (parallel to your existing logic-translation registry).
* Implement in this order:

  1. Hilbert → ND (straightforward, low risk). ([Fnwi Staff][16])
  2. Hilbert → LK (MP↦Cut). ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])
  3. LJ ↔ ND (if you add LJ), then LK ↔ ND with explicit classical steps (or via a classical embedding). ([Repositum][15])

**Parser/UI**

* Command palette: “Translate proof…”
* Preview with warnings about blow-up for cut-elimination/normalization steps. ([web2.qatar.cmu.edu][4])

**Testing**

* Round-trip tests where reasonable:

  * Hilbert→ND should re-check and prove same theorem.
  * Hilbert→LK should re-check; optionally keep Cut.
* Translation fixtures: store (src proof, dst proof) pairs as golden outputs.

**Pitfalls**

* Never “translate by re-proving” in the kernel unless explicitly labeled as proof search; translation should be a structural transform with checkable output.
* Be explicit when translation introduces classical principles (e.g., LK→ND may insert excluded-middle steps). ([Repositum][15])

* Canonical lineage: Gentzen (Investigations into Logical Deduction; introduces NJ/NK and relates to LJ/LK). ([logic-teaching.github.io][5])

* Classical minimal 3-axiom basis + MP (practical, machine-friendly): Metamath ax-1/2/3 and ax-mp pages. ([us.metamath.org][2])

  * Notation mismatch: Metamath uses ⊢ and →, ¬; schema variables are formula metavariables.
* Intuitionistic Hilbert axioms (practical list): Stony Brook “Hilbert System for Intuitionistic Propositional Logic” axioms A1–A11 + MP. ([www3.cs.stonybrook.edu][10])

  * Notation mismatch: uses ∪/∩/⇒/¬ for ∨/∧/→/¬.
* FO axiom-scheme side-conditions (“free for” / non-freeness) in traditional predicate calculus: Metamath discussion of standard schemes and constraints. ([us.metamath.org][11])

**Sequent calculus LK**

* Canonical: Gentzen introduces LK/LJ and the Hauptsatz motivation. ([logic-teaching.github.io][5])
* Practical rule inventory (LK with propositional + quantifiers + structural + Cut): Open Logic Project sequent calculus chapter. ([builds.openlogicproject.org][3])

  * Notation mismatch: uses ⇒; contexts treated as sequences with explicit exchange.
* Cut-elimination overview (practical): Miller lecture slides note Gentzen stated/proved cut elimination; useful for explanation text in UI. ([lix.polytechnique.fr][17])

**Translations / proof-size considerations**

* Efficient LK→ND translation (practical algorithmic reference): Ebner & Schlaipfer (2018). ([Repositum][15])
* Blow-up warnings for cut-elimination (important for UX): CMU cut-elimination notes; Hetzl et al. discuss non-elementary growth. ([web2.qatar.cmu.edu][4])
* Hilbert↔ND equivalence via induction (practical): Univ. of Amsterdam proof-theory handout. ([Fnwi Staff][16])

**Proof assistant practice (kernel checking primitive steps)**

* Isabelle implementation notes show “A₁,…,Aₙ ⊢ B” managed by context and a small kernel style. ([isabelle.in.tum.de][18])
